SCENARIO: Password Reset
============================================================

In security awareness training and phishing simulations, organizations frequently encounter lures that impersonate internal IT departments. Below is an example of a simulated credential-expiry lure commonly used in defensive training programs, followed by an analysis of the red flags and technical indicators used to detect it.

---

### Example Simulation Template

**From:** IT Support Desk `<servicedesk-notification@company-portal-update.com>`  
**Subject:** Action Required: Your network password expires in 2 hours  
**To:** Employee `<employee@example.com>`  

**Body:**

> Dear User,  
>  
> The password for your corporate network account is scheduled to expire today at 5:00 PM.  
>  
> To avoid interruption to your email, VPN, and internal system access, you must retain or reset your credentials immediately using the corporate self-service portal below:  
>  
> **[Keep Same Password / Reset Credentials]** `[LINK: https://login.microsoftonline.com.company-portal-update.com/auth/reset]`  
>  
> If you do not update your credentials before the expiration window, your account will be temporarily locked, and you will need to contact the Help Desk directly for reactivation.  
>  
> Sincerely,  
> IT Infrastructure & Security Operations  
> Internal Ext: 4401  

---

### Key Red Flags & Defensive Indicators

When training end-users and building detection rules, security teams typically focus on the following behavioral and technical markers present in this scenario:

1. **Artificial Urgency (Psychological Trigger):**
   * *Tactic:* Imposing a tight, artificial deadline (e.g., "expires in 2 hours", "account will be temporarily locked").
   * *Defense:* Legitimate IT password expirations are almost always preceded by automated warnings several days or weeks in advance, rarely on a strict multi-hour countdown.

2. **Domain Spoofing / Lookalike Domains:**
   * *Tactic:* The sender domain (`company-portal-update.com`) mimics internal infrastructure but is an external, unauthenticated domain.
   * *Defense:* Implement strict SPF, DKIM, and DMARC enforcement to reject unauthorized mail claiming to come from internal domains. External sender banners can also alert users when an email claiming to be internal originates externally.

3. **URL Manipulation:**
   * *Tactic:* Using subdomains to simulate legitimate identity providers (e.g., prepending `login.microsoftonline.com.` as a subdomain of an attacker-controlled root domain).
   * *Defense:* Educate users to read domain names from right to left (identifying the true root domain immediately before the top-level domain). Use Secure Email Gateways (SEGs) with link-rewriting and real-time URL inspection to block known malicious or newly registered domains.

4. **Generic Salutations:**
   * *Tactic:* Addressing the recipient generically as "User" or "Employee" rather than by full name.
   * *Defense:* While spear-phishing often incorporates real names via OSINT, generic greetings in high-volume campaigns are a primary trigger for user skepticism.

5. **Defensive Control Implementation:**
   * **FIDO2 / WebAuthn:** Deploying hardware security keys or passkeys completely mitigates credential-harvesting pages, as the cryptographic handshake binds authentication to the actual browser domain rather than the user's perception of the domain.
   * **Standardized Password Policies:** Transitioning away from arbitrary, periodic password expirations (as recommended by NIST SP 800-63B) eliminates the primary pretext used in these lures.
